Where to find help in India

Organisations and details below are current as of August 2026. This page is updated as things change, which a printed book cannot be. Your own care team will always know what is available locally.

Patient and parent communities

The Blue Circle Diabetes Foundation is India’s largest patient-led diabetes community, running support networks, awareness work and resources for people with type 1 and their families. Many cities also have local parent groups for type 1 on WhatsApp and social media; your care team or a diabetes educator can point you to one near you.

Insulin and supply assistance

The Changing Diabetes in Children (CDiC) programme provides free insulin, supplies, monitoring and education to children with type 1 from families in financial hardship, through partner hospitals across India. Ask your paediatric diabetes team whether a nearby centre takes part, and about other charitable and hospital-based schemes.

Diabetes camps for children

Residential and day camps, run by paediatric diabetes centres and foundations across the country, let children with type 1 meet others like them, often for the first time. Ask your team which camps operate in your region.

Specialist care

For a paediatric endocrinologist or diabetologist experienced in childhood type 1, the Indian Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Endocrinology (ISPAE) and the Research Society for the Study of Diabetes in India (RSSDI) are the professional bodies whose members provide such care.

Government support

Central and state programmes for children with chronic conditions, including the National Programme for Prevention and Control of Non-Communicable Diseases (NP-NCD) and the Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK) child-health scheme, may offer help. What applies varies by state, so ask your hospital’s social worker or your care team what you are entitled to where you live.

Reliable information

For evidence-based reading that steers you away from misinformation, trustworthy international bodies include the International Society for Pediatric and Adolescent Diabetes (ISPAD), Breakthrough T1D (formerly JDRF), and Beyond Type 1, alongside the Indian bodies above.

If your mind needs support

Type 1 is a weight on the whole family. India’s free government mental-health helpline, Tele-MANAS, can be reached at 14416 or 1-800-891-4416, at any time of day.

If your child is unwell right now, vomiting, drowsy, breathing abnormally, or you are worried in any way, contact your care team or emergency services immediately. Do not consult a book or a web page.

Never reduce or stop insulin on the basis of anything you read here or anywhere else. That decision belongs to your care team alone.
