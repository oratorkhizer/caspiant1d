LADA: type 1 diabetes
that starts in adults.

LADA stands for latent autoimmune diabetes in adults. Like type 1 diabetes, the immune system slowly damages the cells that make insulin. But it starts in adults and moves slowly, so tablets often work at first, and many people are told they have type 2 diabetes. Over months to years, their own insulin falls. Getting the label right changes the treatment and lowers the risk of serious problems.

Clues

When to suspect LADA

Diagnosed as an adult

Often after 30, sometimes with a normal or low weight. LADA can also occur in people with overweight.

Tablets stop working

Sugars get harder to control within a few years, despite good effort and more tablets.

Weight loss without trying

Losing weight while sugars rise is a sign that insulin is running short.

Other autoimmune conditions

Thyroid disease, vitiligo or coeliac disease, in you or close family.

Type 1 in the family

A parent, sibling or child with type 1 diabetes.

Ketones or a DKA scare

Ketones in urine or DKA, which is unusual in type 2.

Tests

How LADA is confirmed

  • GAD antibodies. The main test. A positive result shows the diabetes is autoimmune. Other antibodies (IA-2, ZnT8) are sometimes added.
  • C-peptide. Shows how much insulin your own pancreas still makes. It guides treatment and is repeated over time.
  • Your history. Age, weight, family history, and how the sugars have behaved on treatment.
Treatment

How LADA is treated

An international expert panel suggests basing treatment on the C-peptide level. This is a summary for understanding, not a prescription.

C-peptideRoughly in ng/mLUsual approach
Below 0.3 nmol/Lbelow 0.9Treat like type 1 diabetes, with insulin several times a day.
0.3 to 0.7 nmol/L0.9 to 2.1Diabetes medicines as per guidelines, with insulin often considered. Recheck C-peptide regularly.
Above 0.7 nmol/Labove 2.1Treat much like type 2 diabetes, with regular C-peptide checks.

Medicines that push the pancreas to make more insulin, such as sulphonylureas, are usually avoided, because they may use up the remaining insulin-making cells faster. Heart, kidney and weight benefits of modern diabetes medicines still matter.

Do not change your own medicines. If you think you may have LADA, bring your reports to your doctor. Do not stop or switch tablets or insulin on your own.
Why it matters

Why the right label matters

  • Insulin started at the right time protects you from very high sugars and DKA.
  • It ends the blame. Rising sugars in LADA are not because you tried less hard.
  • Family members can be told about their own risk of autoimmune diabetes and thyroid disease.
  • It opens the door to tools used in type 1 diabetes, such as CGM, carb counting and, where it fits, an insulin pump.

Questions

What families ask us

Is LADA type 1 or type 2 diabetes?

LADA is a form of autoimmune diabetes, closer to type 1. It is sometimes called type 1.5 because it starts slowly in adults, and tablets may work at first, like type 2.

Will I need insulin if I have LADA?

Most people with LADA need insulin eventually. When depends on how much insulin your own body still makes, which is measured with the C-peptide test and repeated over time.

Which test confirms LADA?

A positive GAD antibody test in an adult with diabetes is the main test. C-peptide is measured alongside it to guide treatment.

Can people with overweight have LADA?

Yes. LADA is more common in people with a normal weight, but it can occur in people with overweight or obesity too.

Can LADA be reversed or cured?

No. LADA is autoimmune and the loss of insulin-making cells continues over time. Good treatment helps keep sugars safe and lowers the risk of complications.

Can tablets be used in LADA?

In early LADA, when C-peptide is still reasonably high, some diabetes medicines may be used, guided by your doctor. Medicines that push the pancreas harder, such as sulphonylureas, are usually avoided.

Who should be tested for LADA?

Adults with diabetes who are not overweight, whose sugars are getting harder to control on tablets, who lose weight without trying, or who have other autoimmune conditions or type 1 diabetes in the family. Your doctor will decide.
